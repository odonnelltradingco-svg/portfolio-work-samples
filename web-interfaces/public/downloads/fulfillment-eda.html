01 / THE QUESTION

What deserves attention?

The task is to clean an order dataset, explain quality decisions and produce useful descriptive analysis. The sample contains deliberate duplicates, invalid required values, missing categories, missing shipping times and one valid extreme order.

I created the reproducible input, reusable cleanup and analysis modules, executed notebook, three figures, review output and findings. Source records are preserved. Every input record is accounted for.

02 / THE DECISIONS

Make the assumptions visible.

  • Invalid required values and later duplicate IDs go to review with original values and reasons.
  • Missing channels stay in the analysis as Unknown.
  • Missing shipping times stay missing; each comparison shows its observed sample size.
  • Money is aggregated as integer cents.
  • Paid, pending and refunded orders remain separate.
  • Large values are flagged using the upper IQR fence and retained, so their influence stays visible.

03 / THE FINDINGS

What this sample shows.

  • 243 input records reconcile to 233 accepted and 10 held for review; no input row disappeared.
  • 180 paid orders total $30,817.65. The median is $79.80, compared with a mean of $171.21.
  • The largest valid paid order is $9,999.00, or 32.4% of paid value. It remains included. 18 paid orders exceed the upper IQR review fence.
  • Shipping is observed for 170 of 180 paid orders; 10 shipping times are missing. Medians use the observed subset only.
  • 13 accepted orders have an unknown channel. They remain in the counts and totals.

These are results of a seeded fictional dataset, not business achievements. The generation rules create some patterns intentionally. No causal effect, population estimate or forecast is claimed. Paid order value is not profit or net cash flow.
